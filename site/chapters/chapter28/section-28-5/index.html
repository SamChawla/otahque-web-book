Checkpoint: Quiz and Challenge

Quiz

Que. 3 What does the sessionid cookie hold, and where is the rest of the session?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. Only the session’s key, 32 random characters. The data, such as who is signed in and places_only, is in a row of the django_session table, found by that key.

Que. 4 Why does the event_list view write to the session only when places is in the address?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. Writing creates a session row and sets a cookie. Writing only when Raj changes the filter means an ordinary visit saves nothing, so visitors who never use the filter get no session.

Que. 5 Raj signs out. What happens to his filter and to otahque_tz, and why?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. The filter is gone, because signing out flushes the session, row and cookie. otahque_tz stays: it is a cookie of its own, written by the browser, and holds nothing about Raj.

Que. 6 Someone sets otahque_tz to Mars/Base. What do they see, and what can they change for anyone else?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. Times in UTC, because the server accepts only real zone names. Nothing for anyone else: the cookie affects only how times are shown to the browser that sent it.

Que. 7 Why does UtcCleaning put the forms’ checks under UTC, not under the event’s zone?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. UTC has no clock changes, so Django’s field stage never refuses a typed time, and in_zone() keeps the one real check in the event’s zone, with Otahque’s own messages. Reading under the event’s zone would check the same thing twice, with two different messages.

Mini-Challenge

Que. 8 Predict what Sam sees after saving his profile: on the page the redirect leads to, and after he reloads it. Is the message waiting in a cookie in between? Then check with l2_messages.py below, run with uv run python manage.py shell -c "exec(open('l2_messages.py').read())".

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. The message waits in the messages cookie on the redirect, appears on the next page, and is gone after a reload, because showing it used it up. The file undoes its work, so your rows are unchanged:

l2_messages.py (practice file; delete it afterwards)

from django.db import transaction
from django.test import Client
from django.test.utils import setup_test_environment

from accounts.models import User

setup_test_environment()
with transaction.atomic():
    demo = User.objects.create_user(
        'demo@example.org', display_name='Demo'
    )
    web = Client()
    web.force_login(demo)
    sent = web.post('/accounts/profile/', {
        'display_name': 'Demo K', 'timezone': '',
    })
    print('waiting in a cookie:', 'messages' in sent.cookies)
    for visit in ('first page', 'after a reload'):
        page = web.get(sent['Location']).content.decode()
        print(f'{visit}:', 'Profile saved.' in page)
    transaction.set_rollback(True)

Output

waiting in a cookie: True
first page: True
after a reload: False

What We Learned

  • A cookie is a value the browser sends back; a session keeps the data on the server and gives the browser only its key.
  • Django’s session cookie is HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax, and lasts two weeks, or until the browser closes when Remember me is clear.
  • A message waits in a signed cookie for one request and is used up when shown; a close button only hides it.
  • A session holds what may be lost; registrations and places are always read from the database.
  • A value the browser writes is untrusted input: check it against a known list.
  • Middleware can choose the time zone for every request; its place in the list matters, and part of the site can be left out.
  • Forms read typed times in the current zone, so Otahque checks its event forms under UTC and reads times in the event’s zone.
  • Two zones show the same time when their offsets from UTC are equal at that moment.
  • Signed values can be read but not changed; they expire only if you ask, can be replayed, and survive a key change only with a fallback.
