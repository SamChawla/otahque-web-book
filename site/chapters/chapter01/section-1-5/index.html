1.5 Putting it together

We began with a workshop and a question: who is actually attending? Duplicate submissions and a cancellation made a simple spreadsheet count misleading. That problem gave us a reason to build Otahque.

We then met the people involved. Sam organizes the workshop. Sai needs confirmation, Raj needs to cancel, and Soni wants enough information to decide whether to register. Their tasks help us choose what the application should do.

What we have decided

Our first version serves one community running free events, with individual registrations. It should connect event publication, registration, confirmation, cancellation, and the organizer’s current attendee list into one working journey.

People’s responsibilities have boundaries. An attendee manages their own participation. An organizer manages the events they are responsible for. Being an organizer for one event does not grant control over every event.

What we have not decided yet

We still need detailed rules for closing times, registering again after cancellation, and competing requests for the last place. We have not chosen the database design or written Django code.

These are deliberate next steps. Chapter 2 will turn the business needs into product requirements, user stories, and acceptance criteria: clear conditions that describe when a behaviour is correct.

A final workshop exercise

Questions and answers

Que. 1 What would a useful problem statement say?

Answer. Separate messages and duplicate submissions make the current attendee count difficult to trust. Attendees also need a clear record of whether they have a place.

Que. 2 What outcome should guide the first version?

Show answerHide answer

Answer. Sam can rely on the current attendee list, while each attendee can check and manage their own participation.

Que. 3 Which question remains open?

Show answerHide answer

Answer. One example is how to handle two people requesting the last place. The rule must prevent the confirmed attendee count from exceeding the event’s capacity.

Que. 4 Are we ready to design database tables?

Show answerHide answer

Answer. We have enough context to discuss the product, but we still need its detailed rules. Those rules will guide the data we store and the checks we implement.
