Checkpoint: Quiz and Challenge

Quiz

Que. 21 What is the one question Otahque must be able to answer reliably?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. Who is attending each event, from one current registration record that the attendee’s actions update.

Que. 22 Sam organizes one event and attends another. What changes between them?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. His role, and so his permissions: he may update the event he organizes, but only his own registration for the event he attends.

Que. 23 Name one thing the first version includes and one thing it defers.

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. It includes free individual registration for one community; it defers, for example, payments and waiting lists.

Que. 24 Is “send me a spreadsheet every evening” a business requirement?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. No. It is a proposed solution. The requirement behind it is that the organizer can rely on the current attendee list.

Que. 25 What makes a requirement checkable?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. An observable result, such as Raj seeing “Cancelled” and Sam’s list no longer counting him.

Mini-Challenge

Review the brief you wrote in Step 3. Underline its business outcome, circle one scope boundary, and mark one open question. Revise one sentence that names a feature without explaining the need it serves. Keep the original beside the revised version so you can explain the change.

Que. 26 What would a useful review and revision show?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. The outcome might be that organizers can rely on the current registration list; the boundary is one community with free individual registration; an open question is the cancellation deadline. Replace “send a spreadsheet” with the outcome that the organizer needs current registrations for planning. The revision should clarify the need without silently deciding the open policy.

What We Learned

  • Otahque exists to give one current answer to “who is attending?”, replacing separate tools that must be reconciled by hand.
  • People have needs and play roles; a role depends on the situation, and permissions follow from the role on a particular event.
  • The first version is small but complete: one community and free individual registration, with later work written down.
  • A business requirement states an outcome; functional requirements and examples make it observable.
  • Open questions are recorded, not guessed; Chapter 2 turns them into product rules.
