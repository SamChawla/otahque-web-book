Checkpoint: Quiz and Challenge

Quiz

Que. 21 What is the difference between a need and a proposed solution?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. A need is what a person is trying to achieve, such as planning from current registrations. A proposed solution is one way to meet it, such as a nightly spreadsheet; there may be better ones.

Que. 22 Write the three parts of a Given/When/Then example and what each one holds.

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. Given: the starting situation. When: the action. Then: the result anyone can observe.

Que. 23 Is a request at exactly 18:00 accepted when registration closes at 18:00?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. No. The window includes the opening instant and excludes the closing one, so 17:59:59 is accepted and 18:00 is refused.

Que. 24 What is an invariant, and which one protects capacity?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. A condition that must always remain true. Active registrations never exceed capacity, whatever the timing of the requests.

Que. 25 In the traceability register, when may evidence be recorded?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. Only after a check has actually run against an implementation, with its date and source. A planned check is not evidence.

Mini-Challenge

On paper, write the acceptance criterion for Raj cancelling after the cancellation deadline has passed, in Given/When/Then form. Then add a row for it to your traceability register: the need, the requirement, a control, and the planned check.

Que. 26 What would a good criterion and register row say?

Flip to check your answerFold answer back

Answer. Given Raj has an active registration and the cancellation deadline for the event has passed, When he asks to cancel, Then his registration stays active, no place is released, and he sees a message explaining that the deadline has passed. Register row: need, Raj can tell his current result; requirement, cancellation only before the deadline; control, the server checks the deadline before changing the registration; planned check, a cancellation attempt after the deadline leaves the registration and the active count unchanged. The evidence column stays empty until that check runs.

What We Learned

  • Stakeholders, needs, outcomes, constraints, and open questions go into a BRD that others can review.
  • A first release is small but complete: it reaches real outcomes, and its deferred work is written down with reasons.
  • Stories say who needs what and why; acceptance criteria say what anyone can observe when the behaviour is right, including refusals.
  • Product rules need their edges: the closing instant, the last place, repeated actions, and who may change what.
  • Quality expectations need a measure, and the PRD gathers behaviour, rules, quality, and open decisions in one place.
  • Traceability links each requirement back to its need and forward to controls, checks, and evidence that exists only after a check runs.
