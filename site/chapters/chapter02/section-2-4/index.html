2.4 Product rules and edge cases

Sam’s workshop has twenty places. Nineteen are already taken. Two members each see one place remaining and request it. Both requests look reasonable on their own. The product must still avoid confirming twenty-one people.

A product rule states a condition the product must enforce consistently. An edge case is a situation near a boundary or outside the most straightforward path: the last place, the exact closing instant, or a repeated cancellation. These cases belong in requirements because they affect what a correct result means.

Separate fixed boundaries from proposed policies

The project already requires one active registration per member and event, capacity protection, personal ownership, and access limited to assigned organizers. The following timing and re-registration policies are a proposed working specification for review. They make our examples precise without claiming that real stakeholders have approved them. We have not assigned actual event deadlines.

An active registration means a currently confirmed place that consumes capacity. A cancelled registration does not consume capacity. Neither status proves physical attendance, and neither says that a person received a meal, a certificate, or any other item. Those are different facts for later workflows.

Opening and closing boundaries

Proposed policy: each event has three times: when registration opens, when it closes, and a cancellation deadline. Opening must be earlier than closing. Closing and the cancellation deadline must be no later than the event’s start. The organizer must supply valid values before publication. The exact choices depend on the event and remain to be agreed.

Registration is allowed at or after opening and strictly before closing. At the closing instant, it is closed. Cancellation is allowed strictly before its deadline. These decisions use Otahque’s own clock, not the clock on a member’s phone or laptop, so changing the time on a device must not extend a deadline.

For an illustrative event whose registration closes at 18:00 in its stated timezone, a request processed at 17:59:59 is before closing; one processed at 18:00 is not. This is an example of the boundary, not a deadline selected for Otahque’s events. A page opened earlier is not a promise that registration remains open when the request is processed.

Cancellation may remain open after registration closes if the configured times allow it. Raj can then release a place, but that release does not reopen registration. A place being free and a place being available to book are different conditions.

Count places, not clicks

Capacity is a positive whole number of places. Available capacity is the configured capacity minus the number of active registrations. For twenty places and nineteen active registrations, one place remains. A request that is refused must not change that count.

An invariant is a condition that must always remain true. Our capacity invariant is that the active registration count never exceeds capacity. Another invariant is that a member has at most one active registration for the same event. Repeated clicks, retried requests, and simultaneous requests must preserve both.

When two people compete for the last place, at most one can receive a new confirmed registration. If both requests are otherwise eligible and processing succeeds, one succeeds and the other receives a full-event result. We do not promise that the person who clicked first on their own device wins the place. An unexpected failure must not result in two confirmations.

Cancellation and registering again

A successful cancellation changes an active registration to cancelled and releases exactly one place. Repeating the cancellation leaves it cancelled and releases no additional place. If Raj retries after the deadline but his earlier cancellation already succeeded, show him the current cancelled result; do not pretend a second cancellation occurred.

Proposed re-registration policy: a member may request a place again after cancellation only when the ordinary registration conditions still hold, including an open window and spare capacity. A previous place is not reserved. The product retains enough history to explain the cancellation and the return, while allowing at most one active registration. Whether one record changes status or several records represent that history is a later design question.

If a cancellation and a new registration happen close together, the product must use a consistent count. Soni may obtain the released place only after Raj’s cancellation has taken effect. These are concurrent actions: actions whose processing overlaps in time. We have described the required outcome without choosing the database technique; Chapter 33 returns to it.

Event changes and authority

Only an assigned organizer may perform the ordinary event-management actions. Reducing capacity below the current active count must be rejected; silently removing members is not an acceptable way to make the count fit. A draft event must not accept registrations. Revoking organizer access must stop future attempts to manage that event.

Some decisions need more than a convenient default. We must agree how event cancellation works, which published details may change after people register, and how affected members are informed. We must also define organizer appointment, exceptional administrator actions, and any permitted corrections. These are explicit release blockers for the affected workflows, not permission to improvise in code. Until they are resolved, a draft test for changing an event must not claim that these policies are complete.

Que. 1 Raj cancels when registration is already closed. Can Soni automatically take his place?

Show answerHide answer

Answer. No. Cancellation releases capacity, but a new registration still needs an open registration window. Free capacity does not override the closing rule.

Que. 2 The same cancellation arrives twice. How many places are released?

Show answerHide answer

Answer. One. The first successful change releases the place. The repeated request reports the cancelled state without changing capacity again.

Try it yourself

A fictional event has capacity twenty and nineteen active registrations. Raj, who holds one of those nineteen places, cancels successfully. Two other eligible members then register successfully while registration remains open. Calculate the active count and available capacity after each step.

Que. 3 What counts should we expect?

Show answerHide answer

Answer. Initially: nineteen active and one free. After Raj cancels: eighteen active and two free. After one new registration: nineteen active and one free. After the second: twenty active and none free. A further request must not increase the count.
