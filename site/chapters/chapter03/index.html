CHAPTER THREE

How the web works

Chapter 2 described what Otahque should do for its visitors, members, and organizers. Before we build those behaviours, we need to understand how a browser asks a website for information and receives a result. We will follow familiar event journeys and give names to the parts involved.

By the end of the chapter, you will be able to:

  • explain the roles of a browser and a web server;
  • identify the main parts of a web address and explain how a browser reaches a site;
  • distinguish asking to read information from requesting a change;
  • describe which work belongs in the browser and which belongs on the server;
  • trace an Otahque journey through requests, decisions, and responses.

You will produce an annotated walkthrough of an event visit and a registration request. You need Chapters 1–2 and familiarity with opening a page in a browser. This chapter explains the intended application; our first local development page comes in Chapter 6, and our first Otahque page in Chapter 9.
